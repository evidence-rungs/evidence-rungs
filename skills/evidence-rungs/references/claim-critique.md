# Optional: a supported methodological caveat

Status: **not yet measured; host/model behavior untested.** Use when the user asks for criticism or a methodological limitation matters to the claim. Do not add a ritual critique to every answer.

State which material was read before criticizing the method. Identify the report/version, the opened part and the reading depth: abstract, selected methods, limitations, or full report. Abstract-only access cannot support a full-method critique. An unreported design detail is unknown, not proof that the researchers omitted it.

Lead with the decision-changing weakness; add another only if it independently changes the answer. Connect the actual design to a particular risk of distortion and explain the condition under which it could alter the interpretation. Avoid a generic complaint about sample size, bias or missing controls that the available text does not establish.

Separate an author-stated limitation from your own inference. Attribute an author caveat to its passage; mark a reasoned concern as an inference and give its evidence and uncertainty. Do not turn a possible limitation into a diagnosis, an allegation, or a finding that the study is invalid. If the decisive section cannot be read, report that access boundary and leave the critique open.

Use the core call/time limits. No additional search is required by this note, and there is no minimum number of criticisms, papers or citations. If no supported caveat changes the answer, omit it.
