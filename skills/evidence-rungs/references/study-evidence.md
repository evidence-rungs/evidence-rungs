# Optional: study identity and search scope

Status: **not yet measured; host/model behavior untested.** Use for a question about studies, not routine fact navigation. This is guidance to try, not evidence of improved research quality.

## What is being counted?

A database record describes an item; a report presents results; a study is the underlying investigation. Connect records and reports to their underlying study before counting independent evidence. A registry entry, preprint, journal article and later results paper can describe the same investigation rather than four confirmations.

Use declared registry/manuscript identifiers, participant groups, recruitment dates and explicit cross-references where available. Similar titles alone do not establish identity. When linkage is uncertain, retain a possible relationship without merging. Keep a genuinely separate cohort or replication separate; a follow-up may share participants without answering the same outcome question. State whether a total counts records, reports or studies, and preserve the uncertainty in that total.

## What was actually retrieved?

A ranked or capped sample is not a systematic or exhaustive search. State the database/provider, query scope, retrieval date and visible limit. If the provider reports more matches than it returns, describe the accessible subset rather than treating the unseen remainder as screened. A missing item in that subset cannot establish that a study or result does not exist.

Work from already available evidence first. No source-count quota and no required extra search are introduced. Stop at the core limits; name an unresolved linkage or incomplete corpus instead of exporting everything, inventing a review protocol, or extending the budget.
